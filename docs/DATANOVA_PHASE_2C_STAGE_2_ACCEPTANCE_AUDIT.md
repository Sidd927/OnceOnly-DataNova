1. **Mathematical Defensibility & Rigor:** All temporal algorithms in `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py` are strictly deterministic, non-parametric, and grounded in robust statistics. No arbitrary heuristics, LLMs, or unstable fitting models are employed.
2. **Leave-One-Out Historical Baseline Invariant:** In `detect_temporal_bursts`, the evaluated observation $t$ is strictly excluded from its own baseline lookback window ($\mathcal{B}_t = \{x_{t - W}, \dots, x_{t - 1}\}$). This guarantees that anomalous bursts do not self-inflate their own baseline thresholds.
3. **Variability Collapse Guardrail:** Median Absolute Deviation ($\text{MAD}$) is properly scaled by $1.4826$ (the asymptotic consistency factor for normal distributions). When $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} = 0$ (e.g. constant sequences or zero-inflated distributions), dispersion safely falls back to sample standard deviation $s$; when $s = 0$, it falls back to a positive floor threshold ($\tau_{\text{tonnes}} = 10.0\text{ t}$, $\tau_{\text{records}} = 1.0$), eliminating any possibility of degenerate zero-threshold burst false positives.
4. **Continuous Calendar Invariant:** Downstream rolling trends, burst detection, and gap analysis operate on continuous calendar periods generated by `generate_calendar_periods`. Zero-activity periods are explicitly populated with zero counts ($0$ records, $0.0\text{ t}$), preventing distorted rolling means from missing months.

## 3. Deep-Dive Mathematical & Algorithmic Audit

### 3.1 Date Normalization (`parse_date`)
The auditor reviewed lines 27–52 of `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py`:
- Accepts `date`, `datetime`, and string representations.
- Rejects empty, whitespace-only, `"null"`, and `"none"` strings without throwing exceptions.
- Gracefully parses standard ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` and alternative `YYYY/MM/DD`.
- Unparseable strings return `None`.

### 3.2 Continuous Calendar Sequence Generation (`generate_calendar_periods`)
The auditor reviewed lines 183–233 of `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py`:
- **Daily:** $P_k = [d_k, d_k]$, advancing 1 day until $d_k > D_{\text{end}}$.
- **Weekly:** Aligns to Monday of the week: $M_k = D_{\text{start}} - \text{weekday}(D_{\text{start}})$. Sunday is $M_k + 6\text{ days}$. Formats label as `f"{iso.year}-W{iso.week:02d}"`. Advances 7 days until $M_k > D_{\text{end}}$. All days within the week share the exact same ISO year and week number.
- **Monthly:** Starts at `date(year, month, 1)`. Ends at `date(year, month, monthrange(year, month)[1])`. Label formatted as `f"{year:04d}-{month:02d}"`. Month increment correctly handles December-to-January rollover ($M=1, Y=Y+1$).
- Inverted inputs ($D_{\text{start}} > D_{\text{end}}$) return an empty list `[]`. Unsupported period types raise a clean `ValueError`.

### 3.3 Robust Dispersion & Scaling (`calculate_dispersion_mad`)
The auditor reviewed lines 139–180 of `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py`:
- Calculates exact sample median $\tilde{x} = \text{median}(X)$.
- Absolute deviations: $D = \{|x_i - \tilde{x}| : x_i \in X\}$.
- Raw MAD: $\text{MAD}_{\text{raw}} = \text{median}(D)$.
- Consistency scale factor: $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} = 1.4826 \times \text{MAD}_{\text{raw}}$.
- Fallback hierarchy:
  1. If $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} > 0$: $\text{dispersion} = \sigma_{\text{MAD}}$.
  2. If $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} = 0$ (more than 50% of values are identical): computes sample standard deviation $s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}$ for $n \ge 2$.
  3. If $s > 0$: $\text{dispersion} = s$.
  4. If $s = 0$ (all values identical or $n < 2$): $\text{dispersion} = \max(0.001, \tau_{\text{floor}})$.

*Auditor Verification:* This multi-tier fallback mathematically guarantees that dispersion never collapses to zero, preventing false-positive burst triggers when historical volume is stable or zero-dominated.

### 3.4 Leave-One-Out Historical Burst Detection (`detect_temporal_bursts`)
The auditor reviewed lines 393–517 of `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py`:
- Historical slice: `baseline_slice = activity_periods[max(0, t - baseline_window) : t]`.
- **Leave-One-Out Guarantee:** Index $t$ is strictly excluded. The lookback slice contains at most $W$ prior completed periods ending at $t - 1$.
- Minimum history check: If $\text{len}(\text{baseline\_slice}) < \text{min\_history}$ (default 5), the period is marked `has_sufficient_history = False`, `is_burst = False`, and assigned an explanatory note: `"Insufficient historical baseline (H < 5 periods)."`.
- Thresholds:
  - $\text{Threshold}_{\text{records}} = \max(\tau_{\text{rec\_min}}, \tilde{x}_{\text{rec}} + k \cdot \text{dispersion}_{\text{rec}})$
  - $\text{Threshold}_{\text{tonnes}} = \max(\tau_{\text{ton\_min}}, \tilde{x}_{\text{ton}} + k \cdot \text{dispersion}_{\text{ton}})$
- Flags require exceeding both the dynamic threshold and the absolute minimum floor ($\ge 2$ records, $\ge 100\text{ t}$).
- Burst classifications: `"BOTH"`, `"TONNES"`, `"RECORDS"`, or `"NONE"`.
- Detailed, human-readable explanations accompany every observation.

### 3.5 Structural Inactivity Gaps (`analyze_temporal_gaps`)
The auditor reviewed lines 520–585 of `backend/app/analytics/temporal.py`:
- Contiguous sequence of zero-activity periods from index $i$ to $j$:
  - Start date: $P_i.\text{period\_start}$
  - End date: $P_j.\text{period\_end}$
  - Duration days: $(P_j.\text{period\_end} - P_i.\text{period\_start}).\text{days} + 1$
  - Duration periods: $j - i + 1$
- Classification logic:
  - If $i = 0$ or $j = N - 1$: `"DATASET_BOUNDARY"`
  - Elif $\text{duration\_periods} \ge 3$: `"CONFIRMED_INACTIVITY"`
  - Else: `"SPARSE_SOURCE_COVERAGE"`
- Attaches previous and next active period metadata for investigative context.
